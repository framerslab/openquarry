# Orient the map

A map is easiest to read when it is turned the same way as the ground. Turning it that way is called orienting the map.

## How

1. Find two things you can see around you that are also on the map, such as a tower and a bridge.
2. Find where you are standing on the map.
3. Turn the map until the tower on the map points to the real tower and the bridge on the map points to the real bridge.

The map now matches the ground. What is to your left on the map is to your left on the ground. The words on the map may be upside down, and that is fine.

## Example

You stand where two paths meet, with a lake on your right. On the map the lake is drawn to the left of the place where the paths meet, so the map is the wrong way round. Turn the map until a line from the meeting of the paths to the lake on the map points at the real lake. Then check a second thing: one of the paths on the map should run the same way as the real path. When both match, the map is oriented.
